# Postura editorial — cuándo publicar, cómo cubrirlo

(Manual de postura editorial, 2026-08-13.) What to do when a relevant story
also touches an ally, prospect, source or strategic relationship of Playbook.
This is a **decision protocol**, not prose style: it changes the process of
editing, never the facts.

**The stance.** Playbook covers the business of sport even when the story can
be uncomfortable for someone we have — or want — a relationship with. A
commercial or editorial relationship is never, by itself, a reason to ignore
relevant news. But it can demand more review, better evidence and more
precision in the language. The goal: **a reader should not be able to guess
our partners from our coverage.**

Independence does not mean seeking conflict — no hardening a headline,
adjudicating intentions, or "pegarle" to someone to demonstrate criterio.
A relationship does not mean avoiding the story — if the industry needs to
know it, we cover it.

**Incentivo ≠ motivación.** In sensitive stories, explain facts, incentives,
contradictions, power and consequences. Never attribute motivations that
can't be proven.

- Can say: *"La FMF tiene incentivos comerciales para mantener una buena
  relación con FIFA."*
- Needs evidence: *"La FMF respaldó a Infantino para conseguir el Mundial de
  Clubes."*

---

## 1. The 60-second decision

Before discussing tone or the relationship, place the story on two axes:
**how relevant** and **how sensitive for Playbook**.

|  | Sensibilidad baja | Sensibilidad alta |
|---|---|---|
| **Relevancia alta** | PUBLICAR — proceso normal | PUBLICAR + protocolo amarillo — más revisión, misma independencia |
| **Relevancia baja** | No es prioridad; solo si aporta algo propio | Probablemente NO publicar — porque la historia no alcanza el estándar editorial, no por la relación |

- **Relevancia alta**: affects money, power, governance, rights, competition,
  regulation, institutional reputation, or a decision that matters to the
  industry.
- **Sensibilidad alta**: directly involves an ally, an active negotiation, a
  key source, a strategic prospect, or someone relevant in the pipeline.

**The two tests:**

- **Test del aliado** — if we had no relationship with this organization,
  would we publish? If yes, the relationship should not stop it.
- **Test del adversario** — if we were in conflict with this organization,
  would we write it the same way? If not, we may be hardening coverage for
  the wrong reasons.

---

## 2. Protocolo amarillo

Activates when the story is relevant AND touches a strategic relationship.
More sensitivity = more rigor, never less coverage.

1. **Separate the levels.** Mark hecho confirmado / reporte de terceros /
   interpretación / escenario (`voice-and-style.md` §8) — explicitly, before
   drafting.
2. **Identify the most delicate sentence.** Which line would the involved
   party contest hardest, and is the evidence sufficient to sustain it
   exactly as written?
3. **Ask for postura when it's warranted** — accusations, contradictory
   versions, or any attributed motivation. Asking for comment is not ceding
   editorial control.
4. **Second read.** Another person reviews headline, evidence, inferences,
   tone and the Opinión de Playbook.
5. **Test de la llamada.** Can every sentence be defended if the president,
   comms director or CEO involved calls tomorrow?

The headline can be strong and factual — it never needs to be more aggressive
than the evidence. The Opinión explains incentives, control, business or
consequences; **it never turns a possibility into an intention, or a
contradiction into an accusation.**

---

## 3. Publicar, escalar o frenar

- **Publicar** when the story is relevant, the facts are substantiated and
  the interpretation can be defended.
- **Escalar** (to the human editor — in a skill run this means STOP and ask,
  it is the publish gate's business) when there are accusations, legal risk,
  non-public information, direct conflict with an ally, or a strong
  interpretation about motivations. Escalating raises the standard; it is not
  asking permission.
- **Frenar** when the source is weak, indispensable context is missing, the
  accusation outruns the evidence, or the story simply lacks editorial value.

**A relationship MAY change:** how much we review, whether we ask for
postura, who does the second read, how we mark fact vs. interpretation, and
timing when there is no editorial urgency.

**A relationship must NEVER change:** a confirmed fact, a public statement, a
relevant contradiction, or the existence of a story the industry needs to
know.

**Coordinación ≠ aprobación.** The team that owns a relationship can know a
sensitive piece is coming — that helps manage the relationship. It does not
give commercial a veto over editorial.

---

## 4. Checklist final

If a sensitive story passes these six, Playbook can publish it calmly:

1. ¿Es suficientemente importante para nuestra audiencia de sports business?
2. ¿La publicaríamos aunque el protagonista no fuera aliado ni prospecto?
3. ¿Tenemos evidencia para cada afirmación delicada?
4. ¿Estamos explicando incentivos y no inventando motivaciones?
5. ¿El titular y la Opinión de Playbook son más precisos que agresivos?
6. ¿Podemos defender cada línea frente al involucrado sin retractarnos de
   nuestro criterio?

**Regla de congruencia.** Playbook's credibility doesn't depend on never
discomforting an ally. It depends on applying the same editorial standard to
allies, prospects, sources and organizations we have no relationship with.

---

## How a skill run uses this

A drafting session cannot know Playbook's current pipeline of allies and
prospects, so the protocol's trigger is conservative: **if the story's
protagonist is a Mexican/LATAM federation, league, club, sponsor or media
company — or anyone the human has mentioned as a partner — treat sensitivity
as plausible.** Run the two tests, apply protocolo amarillo's steps 1–2 to
the draft, and surface the classification in the draft presentation
("sensibilidad: alta/baja, y por qué") so the human review gate
(`publishing-mechanics.md`) can do steps 3–5 with real knowledge of the
relationships. Never skip a relevant story because it might be sensitive —
that decision belongs to the human, made visible, not silently.
